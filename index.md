## About

Jetshri Bhadviya is a curator, educator, designer, researcher, and new media artist whose work explores the relationship between the human body, space, and its emotional, cultural, and metaphysical dimensions through video, sound, performance, photography, and installation. Born in Udaipur, India, her experiences of migration and cultural displacement inform her investigations of identity, memory, embodiment, belonging, and representation.

Her creative practice examines how bodies, objects, images, rituals, and technologies carry personal and collective histories. Informed by Indian culture and Jain philosophy, she explores gender roles, body politics, religion, surveillance, environmental justice, and the complexities of diasporic identity. She is particularly interested in the body as an archive and in the tension between institutional records of identity and lived experience.

Jetshri is an Assistant Professor in the School of Digital Media Production at Daytona State College. She is pursuing a Ph.D. in Texts and Technology, specializing in Digital Media, at the University of Central Florida. Her doctoral research considers how visual culture, artificial intelligence, and emerging technologies influence identity, authorship, memory, embodiment, and cultural representation. Through practice-based research, she brings photography, performance, sound, installation, and immersive technologies into conversation with scholarly inquiry.

Her curatorial work focuses on identity, immigration, gender politics, environmental concerns, and equitable representation. She has served as an Art and Culture Commissioner for the City of Hamtramck and as a curator for the Detroit Artist Market. She has also worked with Mighty Real/Queer Detroit and served as a Curatorial Assistant in the Contemporary Art Department at the Detroit Institute of Arts.

Her exhibitions have included the Grand Rapids Art Museum, Kresge Arts Foundation, and David Klein Gallery in Michigan, as well as the Sheikha Manal Young Artist Foundation in Dubai, where she was recognized as a top-ten artist in 2011. She received the Toby Devan Lewis Fellowship Award in 2015.

### Art Practice

As an artist, Jetshri explores the layered complexities of personal identity, cultural heritage, and the human experience through photography, performance, sound, and sculpture. Her practice is deeply influenced by her upbringing in Jainism, an ancient Indian tradition centered on non-violence, introspection, and spiritual growth. Her early training in Kathak, a classical Indian dance form, fostered an appreciation for rhythm, storytelling, and discipline. These formative influences continue to shape her exploration of the dynamic balance between tradition and personal expression.

While the principles of restraint and detachment inform her philosophical lens, her artistic work also investigates how social frameworks shape and sometimes limit our sense of self. Through performance and its photographic traces, she builds spaces where conventional identity markers can be momentarily suspended. Whether wrapped in ropes adorned with bells—evocative of cultural expectations—or veiled in translucent fabric forms, her body becomes a medium for inquiry and transformation.

Her photographs are more than documentation; they hold a tension between presence and disappearance. By partially obscuring the figure, she invites viewers to reconsider how identity is interpreted through appearance, origin, and biography, while opening a space for ambiguity and becoming.

Sound is an equally vital element in her work, building immersive sonic environments that mirror and expand the visual. In *#CheckYourMisconceptionsAtTheDoor*, for example, she emerges from a shapeless form as an anonymous voice asks:

> Can you see me?  
> Why do you think I'm here?  
> I am unwilling to disclose my identity.  
> I'm no one.  
> I'm not from here.  
> I'm not from anywhere.

This becomes both a personal statement and an open prompt, inviting the audience to reflect on perception and presence. In an increasingly categorized world, she offers a space for fluidity—where the undefined self can be both powerful and poetic.

### Community Statement

Jetshri is dedicated to cultivating inclusive and engaging spaces that support diverse creative voices and encourage meaningful cultural dialogue. Through her work as a curator, educator, and artist, she aims to build platforms where individuals from a variety of backgrounds can share their stories, ideas, and artistic visions. Whether through exhibitions, performances, or collaborative initiatives, she focuses on creating opportunities for thoughtful engagement around themes of identity, visibility, and cultural exchange. Her goal is to foster understanding and collaboration within the arts and beyond, encouraging open dialogue and shared learning.

### The Educator

With over a decade of teaching experience, Jetshri guides students in lens-based media by integrating technical instruction with conceptual development. Her approach is responsive to individual learners, emphasizing creative experimentation and critical inquiry. Beyond the classroom, she connects students with real-world opportunities—including exhibitions, internships, and collaborations—that help them grow as artists and cultural contributors. Her teaching encourages curiosity, resilience, and a sense of purpose, empowering students to shape and participate in contemporary media and visual storytelling.

## Academic Interests

Jetshri's academic and research interests lie at the intersection of photography, digital media, visual culture, critical making, and interdisciplinary artistic research. Through practice-based inquiry, she explores how identity, memory, displacement, cultural narratives, and embodied experiences are constructed, represented, and transformed through artistic practices and emerging technologies. Her research examines the body and objects as archives of personal and collective memory, particularly in relation to diaspora, gender, spirituality, mythology, and cultural inheritance.

She is also interested in the intersections of technology, authorship, ethics, and representation, investigating how artificial intelligence, digital archives, surveillance, and immersive media shape our understanding of identity and lived experience. As an artist, curator, and educator, she approaches creative practice as a form of knowledge production, using material experimentation, critical inquiry, and interdisciplinary methodologies to challenge dominant narratives and explore alternative ways of knowing.

- Environmental Humanities, the Anthropocene, and Ecological Justice
- Interdisciplinary Art and Design Pedagogy
